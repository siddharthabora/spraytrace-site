Today the only record is the number of tanks issued

The manager sets the chemical, dose and sections. Tanks are filled at the mixing point and the supervisor moves between groups, checking a few workers at a time. Whether each section got the right litres, evenly, rests on each worker's effort.

Tanks dumped

A full tank emptied in one corner looks the same in the register as a tank sprayed across the section.

Strips skipped

Part of a section goes unsprayed or gets sprayed twice, and nobody sees it until pests come back.

Doses off the programme

Too much in a small patch, too little elsewhere: higher chemical bills, uneven control and residue risk for made tea.

Wires and nozzles tampered

A loosened wire or a fiddled nozzle is the first thing a worker on fixed pay reaches for to finish early.

Found days later, misuse is easy to deny. The estate manager we worked with at Bokakhat was clear that an alert only helps if it reaches the supervisor while the worker is still there.

How a spray day runs with SprayTrace

Workers spray as they do now. The only new task for anyone is answering an alert.

  1. Issue at the mixing point

    Each tank is linked to a worker and a section. A tank resting in the refill zone for about a minute counts as a fill.

  2. Spray as usual

    The tag wakes on motion, counts lever strokes and how far each one swings, and logs GPS every second.

  3. Report every 10 s

    A 26-byte heartbeat goes to the division hub in the tag's own GPS-timed slot. No SIM, no internet.

  4. Check the alert

    The supervisor sees tank, worker, section and a map pin, calls or sends a clerk, then marks it verified or a false alarm.

  5. Close the section

    Coverage, litres per hectare against the programme and skipped strips. Re-spray or sign off, then export the record.

The tag

Palm-sized and sealed. It never touches the spray liquid, so there is nothing on the spray line to cut into or tamper with.

Motion sensorCounts pump-lever strokes and the swing of each one, so short strokes count for less. Litres are strokes times the litres per stroke, calibrated once per sprayer model with weighed water.
GPSLogs the walked track every second and matches it to your section map for coverage and litres per hectare.Section-level accuracy. Consumer GPS cannot resolve individual tea rows.
RadioLoRa in the licence-exempt 865 to 868 MHz band at 21 dBm, one heartbeat every 10 s in its own slot, about 1 to 2% duty.Within India's G.S.R. 853(E) limits of 500 mW e.r.p. and 2.5% duty.
PowerRechargeable cell, charged overnight at the muster point. Target: a full spray day with margin.Estimated at about 13 hours; to be measured on the bench.
HousingSealed case with four tamper screws, strap slots and no external wires. IP65 on the prototype, IP67 in production.
Phase 2A three-point tank-level strip in the same housing to recalibrate litres per stroke and confirm when a tank is emptied.

Prototype: ESP32, MPU6050, u-blox MAX-M10S and Ebyte E32-900T30D. Production targets are design intentions that bench and field tests will confirm.

Alerts, not data dumps

Managers told us they want to know what needs checking, not to read logs. SprayTrace sorts everything into two kinds.

Needs a check now

  • Tag silentNo heartbeat for three slots, about 30 s
  • Over-doseLitres per hectare above the round's alert band
  • Under-dose or skipped areaToo few litres, or strips with no track
  • Fill mismatchA tank back at the mixing point well short of 16 L pumped

For the day report

  • Poor techniqueRushed or short strokes, or a walking pace off target

What we have tested so far

We report what the prototype has shown and what it has not shown yet.

Field run at Bokakhat Tea Estate

In the first half of November 2026 we ran the prototype tag on a working knapsack sprayer, transmitting at 21 dBm to our base station. With untuned thresholds, its stroke count came within 10% of a manual count. It logged a GPS track through the section, and the base station kept receiving packets at 300 m and beyond without any mobile network.

Estate interviews

Managers and supervisors at three estates, two in Bokakhat and one in Jorhat, plus a full spray round observed. Their requirements set the design: no external wires, alerts over data, real-time reporting and a cost ceiling near ₹1,000 per sprayer.

Radio plan checked against the rules

Transmit power, channel width and duty cycle were checked against India's licence-exempt rules for 865 to 868 MHz, and the heartbeat moved to GPS-timed slots so many tags can share one hub.

Not shown yet: collisions with many tags at once, volume accuracy against weighed water and battery sprayers. The next tests use three tags together, a reference sensor on the lever and calibration with weighed water.

Fair to workers by design

SprayTrace tracks where spraying happens, so it has to be fair to the people doing it.

  • Consent first. Notice in Assamese, Hindi or Bengali, with consent recorded under the DPDP Act, 2023.
  • Only during spray shifts. The tag records only while the sprayer is in use and has no camera or microphone.
  • Groups by default. Reports show worker groups; named scores appear only if the estate runs an incentive scheme.
  • The estate's data. In Phase 1 everything stays on the estate's own laptop.
